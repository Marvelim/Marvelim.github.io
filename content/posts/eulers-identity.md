# A note on Euler's identity

Euler's identity ties five fundamental constants together in a single line:

$$e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0.$$

It falls out of the more general formula

$$e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta,$$

by setting $\theta = \pi$. Geometrically, $e^{i\theta}$ is the unit vector at angle $\theta$ on the complex plane; rotating it by half a turn ($\theta = \pi$) lands you exactly at $-1$. That's the whole story.
